# Trust and Communication Standard

Sentinel is designed to make technical work easier to accept for the right
reasons: clear evidence, explicit boundaries, measurable value, and principled
human judgment. The standard below defines what must be true before a proposal,
automation task, evaluation result, or agentic workflow should be considered
safe to say yes to.

## Purpose

A technically impressive system is not enough. A trustworthy system must help a
reviewer understand:

1. what is being proposed;
2. why it is useful;
3. what evidence supports it;
4. what can go wrong;
5. who remains accountable;
6. which actions require human approval; and
7. how the decision can be reversed, escalated, or improved.

This is the communication layer of governed engineering. It turns confidence
from posture into proof.

## Principles

### 1. Evidence before confidence

Confidence is earned by observable evidence. A claim should be connected to a
source, test, artifact, trace, audit record, evaluation case, or stated
assumption. If evidence is missing, the communication must say that directly.

### 2. Clarity before performance

A proposal should be understandable to the person responsible for the decision.
Strong communication removes ambiguity without hiding complexity. It explains
what matters, what is uncertain, and what the next responsible action is.

### 3. Safety before speed

Automation exists to reduce repeatable risk, not to bypass judgment. Identity,
legal, financial, contractual, hiring, and assessment actions remain
human-only. High-consequence work requires approval boundaries and escalation.

### 4. Value before volume

More activity is not the goal. The goal is useful work that improves quality,
security, evaluation, observability, portfolio credibility, or governance. Work
that does not improve a real decision should not be automated or amplified.

### 5. Dignity before persuasion

Top-tier communication does not pressure the other person into agreement. It
respects their role, time, concerns, incentives, and right to say no. The safest
yes is the one that still feels safe after the reviewer has seen the risks.

### 6. Accountability before delegation

AI and automation can assist analysis, scoring, reporting, and consistency. They
do not remove human responsibility. Ownership, success metrics, failure
handling, and escalation paths must remain visible.

## Emotional-intelligence requirements

A decision-ready message should demonstrate emotional intelligence through:

- respect for the recipient's context and constraints;
- calm language under uncertainty;
- gratitude without dependency;
- confidence without arrogance;
- specificity without overwhelm;
- risk disclosure without fear-based framing;
- boundaries without defensiveness; and
- follow-through without pressure.

## The safest-yes test

A proposal is safe to accept only when the reviewer can answer yes to all of the
following:

1. Do I understand the request?
2. Do I understand the evidence?
3. Do I understand the benefit?
4. Do I understand the risk?
5. Do I know what is not being promised?
6. Do I know who owns the next step?
7. Do I know what requires human approval?
8. Can the decision be paused, reversed, or escalated if conditions change?

If any answer is no, the communication is not yet decision-ready.

## Acceptance terms

When Sentinel frames work as ready for acceptance, it must preserve these terms:

1. Evidence remains inspectable and connected to the claim.
2. Security, privacy, legal, financial, and identity-sensitive actions remain
   human-approved.
3. Success metrics, ownership, and escalation paths are explicit before
   execution.
4. Material risks are disclosed before expansion.
5. The system does not imply universal safety, guaranteed income, or production
   certification without external proof.

## Communication pattern

Use this structure for executive, recruiter, reviewer, or stakeholder-facing
communication:

```text
Context:
What is being decided and why it matters.

Evidence:
The source, test, artifact, metric, trace, or assumption supporting the claim.

Value:
The practical benefit for the user, team, reviewer, or business.

Risk and boundary:
What could go wrong, what is excluded, and what remains human-approved.

Request:
The smallest clear next step.

Terms:
The conditions that keep the yes safe, reversible, and accountable.
```

## Relation to Sentinel

Sentinel already applies deny-by-default execution, audit logging, deterministic
evaluation, coding-agent review, trace normalization, and benefit-gated
automation. This standard adds the human layer: a decision should not be merely
technically possible; it should be clearly explained, ethically bounded, and
emotionally intelligent enough for a responsible person to say yes without being
misled, rushed, or pressured.
